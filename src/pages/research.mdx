On the high level, I work on <mark>Numerical Methods for Data Science</mark>; in the recent years I focus specifically on the intersection of **numerical (randomized) linear algebra** and **network science**: I design efficient and provable algorithms for the matrices associated with graphs and their higher-order generalizations, such as simplicial complexes (using Hodge Laplacian operators), and operators used for in **graph signal processing** (graph filters). I am currently part of the [Computational Network Science](https://netsci.rwth-aachen.de) group of **Michael Schaub** at _RWTH Aachen_.

##### Higher-order networks

Simplicial complexes allow to model polyadic interactions, but the Hodge Laplacians that describe them are large, ill-conditioned and expensive to work with. My PhD thesis, <mark>_Topological Instability and Preconditioning of Higher-Order Laplacian Operators on Simplicial Complexes_</mark>, tackled this from two sides. The first question was how stable the topology is: I suggested to reduce the problem of searching for the **smallest perturbation that changes a homology group** as a spectral matrix nearness problem and solved it with a bilevel gradient-flow method (_J. Sci. Comput._, 2023). The second part addressed quicker linear system solutions with these operators: I built a **Cholesky-like preconditioner** from a heavy collapsible subcomplex (_SIMAX_, 2024). This work brought me to the notion of **sparsification of simplicial complexes** using local densities of states (_SIMODS_, 2026) which allows to reduce simplicial complex to a sparser, more computationally efficient one at any order with great computational complexity.

##### Learning Algorithms through the Computational Lense

I aim to bring similar matrix-analytic tools to machine learning. This includes exploiting contractivity of **neural ODEs** as an eigenvalue optimization problem (_Math. Comp._, 2025) for stabilisation, **convergence of gradient descent/flow** for linear GNNs (preprint, 2025), and **Riemannian interpolation of low-rank graph filters** on the Grassmann manifold for parametric graph families (preprint, 2025).

##### Coupled oscillators

Earlier, I worked on inverse problems for Kuramoto and Van der Pol models of **coupled oscillators**, with applications to synchronization in **solar activity** (_Physica D_ 2020, 2024; _CNSNS_ 2020; _Entropy_ 2020).

Going forward, I want to make linear algebra for higher-order and evolving networks scalable and structure-aware. That means exploiting topology to build faster solvers, sparsifiers and filters, and connecting them to learning on relational data.
